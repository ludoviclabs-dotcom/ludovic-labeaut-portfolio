The US government becomes a shareholder: financing industrial power

A strategic plant can be indispensable to national security without offering investors a sufficiently predictable return. To close that gap, Washington takes equity stakes, secures revenues and brings in the banks. The Pentagon now reaches all the way into the financing of its suppliers.

By Ludovic Labeaut4 min readData as of 26 September 2026

This goes beyond an isolated case. In the version of its tracker accessed on 26 September, the Council on Foreign Relations lists 39 deals announced since January 2025, worth $27.7bn. The tracker covers equity stakes and similar instruments: it does not measure a sum that has been fully disbursed. It nonetheless documents the broadening of an industrial policy that had until then relied mainly on subsidies, loans and tax incentives. [1]

01MP Materials: making an industrial supply chain bankable

The partnership with MP Materials announced on 10 July 2025 aims to build a US supply chain for rare earths and permanent magnets. It combines four instruments. [2]

Instrument provided for in the agreement Economic function
$400m in convertible preferred stock, with a warrant Provide capital
$150m loan Expand heavy rare-earth separation
$110/kg floor for neodymium-praseodymium products, for ten years Reduce price risk
Guaranteed ten-year military and commercial offtake for magnets from the future 10X plant Secure demand

JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs also provided a commitment letter for $1bn of financing for 10X, subject to conditions. [2] Our reading: securing future revenues can make a plant more bankable than a one-off injection of funds.

02Missiles extend the trend

On 23 April 2026, L3Harris announced that it had closed a $1bn government investment in its Missile Solutions business. The instrument is a preferred security convertible into common stock upon an initial public offering, accompanied by warrants. The funds are intended to expand and modernise industrial capacity, notably for solid rocket motors. [3]

The financial distinction is essential: the financing has closed, whereas conversion into common stock depends on the event specified in the contract. At the time, the press release pointed to an IPO envisaged for the second half of 2026, subject to market conditions. It is therefore not enough to speak of a “Pentagon cheque”: the government obtains an equity instrument tied to an industrial trajectory.

03The banks are financing sovereignty too

Public capital is part of a broader mobilisation. In October 2025, JPMorganChase announced a ten-year, $1,500bn initiative to facilitate, finance and support investment in strategic sectors: industry, defence, energy and technology. [4]

This figure calls for careful reading. The bank specifies that about $1,000bn was already planned; the additional ambition therefore amounts to up to $500bn. Direct equity and venture capital investments, for their part, account for up to $10bn. The $1,500bn total thus covers several financial activities. It represents neither public funding nor equity stakes already taken. [4]

The mechanism sought is one of crowding in: public commitments make certain projects more predictable, and lenders and investors can then finance them more easily. For the banks, these priorities also open up profitable markets. Strategic and commercial interests can converge without becoming identical.

04Industrial insurance comes at a price

This policy shifts part of the risk onto taxpayers. A price floor can sustain production when the market falls, at a cost to the budget. CSIS highlights this trade-off in its study of support mechanisms for critical minerals. [5] An equity stake also exposes the government to a loss of value if the company fails.

Then there is a question of governance: how can fair competition be preserved when the government becomes at once financier, shareholder and customer? In our view, success should be judged by the capacity actually brought into service, the reduction in dependencies and the public cost per result achieved. A rising market valuation does not, on its own, answer these questions.

For French readers, the subject is concrete. The presentation of France’s economic security doctrine on 24 September 2026 explicitly mentions support for strategic technologies and sectors through subsidies and equity, in compliance with French and European law. [6] The common challenge is to turn financing into lasting productive capacity. Buying a stake is a means; having an industry capable of delivering remains the goal.
